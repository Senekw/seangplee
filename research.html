  • Tumor Phospho-STAT3 and Microenvironmental Heterogeneity Across Gastrointestinal Cancers: A Multiomic Analysis
    ESMO Asia Congress 2026 · Singapore · ePoster 1099eP · Accepted
    Gastrointestinal cancers cause a large share of cancer deaths worldwide. Mapping what a tumor's surroundings look like when pSTAT3 is active may help guide which treatments are worth testing in which patients.
  • Resource Inversion: Oncology Patient Summaries in Low-Resource Languages
    NeurIPS 2026 Workshop GenAI4Health · Accepted (poster)
    OpenReview
    Benchmarks small on-premise language models for writing oncology after-visit summaries in 10 languages spoken by high liver-cancer-burden populations, and routes unverified facts to human interpreters. Many cancer patients get their visit instructions in a language they don't read well. Summaries they can read, with a human interpreter stepping in when the model is unsure, could help them follow their care safely.
  • Resource Inversion: Cross-Lingual Fidelity and Verified Deferral for Oncology Patient Communication in Low-Resource Languages
    NeurIPS 2026 Workshop LP4FM · Accepted (poster)
    OpenReview
    A mistranslated dose or instruction can hurt a patient. This work checks how well medical facts survive translation, so errors are caught and sent to a person before they reach anyone.
  • A 6-Gene pSTAT3 Transcriptomic Score Identifies an Immunosuppressive, Chemotherapy-Resistant Phenotype and Predicts Poor Survival in Biliary Tract Cancer
    The Oncologist · Oxford University Press · Jun 23, 2026 · Published
    The Oncologist
    Develops and validates a six-gene pSTAT3 transcriptomic score in biliary-tract cancer, linking high pathway activity to shorter survival, an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, and chemotherapy resistance. Biliary tract cancer is rare and has few treatment options. A score that flags tumors likely to resist chemotherapy could help find the patients who need a different approach.
  • Tumor Phospho-STAT3 Marks a Shared Angiogenic-Fibrotic, Immune-Replete Microenvironment Across Gastrointestinal Cancers: A Multi-Omic Analysis of 1,274 Tumors
    First-author manuscript · under review at Molecular Oncology · 2026
    Uses genomic, transcriptomic, and spatial data from 1,274 tumors across gastrointestinal cancers to test whether pSTAT3 marks a shared angiogenic-fibrotic, immune-replete microenvironment linked to patient survival. If the same pattern shows up across several gastrointestinal cancers, what is learned in one may carry over to others, including rare ones that are hard to study alone.

Preprints

  • Tissue tearing degrades optimal-transport registration of spatial transcriptomics beyond displacement magnitude: a multiseed deformation benchmark and a supervised graph cross-attention proof-of-concept
    bioRxiv · Jul 5, 2026 · Preprint
    bioRxiv
    Builds a controlled human-DLPFC benchmark showing that tissue tears break optimal-transport, diffeomorphic, and Gaussian-process registration methods; tests a graph cross-attention model and diagnoses donor shift as its main generalization failure. Real tissue samples often tear during preparation. Knowing where alignment tools fail helps researchers avoid drawing wrong conclusions from damaged samples.
  • Most published immunotherapy-response gene signatures do not outperform random gene sets, and collapse onto a single tumor-inflammation axis
    Open Science Framework (OSF) · 2026
    OSF
    Benchmarks 102 published immune-checkpoint response signatures across 15 cohorts against size-matched random gene sets, finding that most collapse onto one tumor-inflammation axis with a held-out predictive ceiling near AUROC 0.59. Immunotherapy helps only some patients and can have serious side effects. Testing the tools meant to predict who benefits shows how much better they still need to be. A related question is now part of my work with Dr. Wilson Goh's lab at NTU.
  • Napabucasin Across Four Registrational Phase 3 Trials in Advanced Gastrointestinal Cancers: A Complete-Enumeration Meta-Analysis Bounding the Survival Effect, and a Tested Non-Replication of the pSTAT3 Biomarker Signal
    OSF Registries · Jul 1, 2026 · Preregistered
    OSF
    Pools all four registrational phase 3 trials of napabucasin, covering 3,383 patients, to bound any clinically meaningful survival benefit and test whether the reported pSTAT3-positive biomarker signal replicates. Thousands of patients took part in these trials. A clear answer on whether the drug and its biomarker hold up helps future trials avoid repeating what did not work.
  • A pan-cancer identifiability atlas for compartment-resolved pathway attribution from bulk transcriptomes
    Open Science Framework (OSF) · 2026
    OSF
    Introduces a calibrated four-part identifiability gate for asking whether bulk RNA can recover malignant-versus-stromal pathway differences; across 5,576 TCGA tumors, only 6.9% of pathway-cohort attributions pass. Studies often say a signal comes from cancer cells or from the tissue around them, using data that cannot always tell the two apart. This shows when that claim can be trusted.
  • Competing risks are ignored in most SEER cancer-specific-mortality nomograms: a systematic audit with real-patient reanalysis and a validated correction pipeline
    Open Science Framework (OSF) · 2026
    OSF
    Audits 1,000 SEER nomogram papers and finds that 82.8% show no evidence of competing-risk handling; validates how naive survival methods overestimate cancer-specific mortality and releases a reproducible correction pipeline. These tools estimate a patient's risk of dying from cancer. Ignoring other causes of death makes that risk look higher than it is, which can mislead patients and doctors.
  • When can a pathway program be attributed to a tumour compartment from bulk RNA? An identifiability audit across immune-checkpoint cohorts
    Open Science Framework (OSF) · 2026
    OSF
    Audits whether malignant and stromal STAT3 activity can be separated from bulk RNA-seq in six immune-checkpoint cohorts; only two pass the identifiability gate, and compartment resolution adds no predictive value beyond the bulk signal. Knowing which part of a tumor a signal comes from shapes what a treatment should target. This helps researchers avoid reading more into the data than it can support.
